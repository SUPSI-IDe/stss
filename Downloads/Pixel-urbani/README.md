# Pixel-urbani

## Description

This folder contains the materials, datasets, and technical resources developed for **Pixel Urbani**, a participatory workshop held from **16–17 July 2025** at **Studio Foce**, Lugano, as part of the **LongLake Festival 2025**, promoted by the **City of Lugano**.

Pixel Urbani brought together citizens, researchers, designers, and actors involved in city governance to explore how urban waste collection is experienced in everyday life. Through a series of collaborative activities centred on observation, documentation, visualization, and discussion, participants reflected on waste-disposal habits, recycling practices, urban infrastructures, and the role of data in understanding the relationship between citizens and the city's waste-management system.

The workshop combined short public interactions with longer collaborative sessions, allowing participants to contribute in different ways and at different levels of engagement. Throughout the activities, observations, mappings, recordings, and reflections generated by participants were progressively transformed into datasets, visualizations, and shared interpretations of urban waste practices in Lugano.

## Folder Contents

### `Data Mapping`

Materials and documentation related to the **Data Mapping** activity.

Participants reconstructed and visualized their waste-disposal routines using printed maps, physical annotations, stickers, strings, pins, and written observations. The activity explored how personal experiences and everyday practices can be translated into collective spatial representations and discussed through participatory mapping.

### `Data Plotting`

Materials, datasets, and software developed for the **Data Plotting** activity.

Using data generated through the workshop, participants explored different visualization approaches to examine urban phenomena from multiple perspectives. The folder includes the data exploration interface, archived datasets, and technical resources supporting collective interpretation and discussion of the collected data.

### `Gesture Tracking`

Materials, datasets, and software developed for the **Gesture Tracking** activity.

Participants recorded simulated waste-disposal gestures through a motion-tracking installation that transformed bodily movements into visual traces and video recordings. The activity explored the physical and experiential dimensions of interacting with urban waste infrastructures, generating an archive of recorded gestures and participant annotations.
