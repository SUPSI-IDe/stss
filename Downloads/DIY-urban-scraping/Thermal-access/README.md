# Thermal-access

## Description

This folder contains the dataset collected with **Thermal-access**, a DIY urban scraping tool designed and developed by students at SUPSI during the **DIY Urban Scraping Summer Workshop**.

Thermal-access uses a thermal imaging sensor to capture the surface temperatures of urban environments. The collected thermal data are algorithmically processed and overlaid onto photographic images, producing visualizations that highlight temperature variations across public space. These manipulated images reveal thermal conditions that are normally invisible to the human eye, drawing attention to patterns of heat exposure, material properties, shading, and accessibility within the urban environment.

The project explores how thermal sensing can support situated observations of urban comfort and environmental conditions through citizen-generated data collection.

## Data Collection Context

The data were collected in **Lugano (Switzerland)** on **16–17 July 2025** during the **Pixel Urbani** workshop, organized within the **Lugano LongLake Festival**.

Data collection took place through a series of guided urban walks involving citizens and workshop participants in the **Foce–Cassarate area**, a lakeside district located at the northeastern edge of Lugano’s city center, characterized by public recreational spaces, pedestrian routes, cultural venues, and access to Lake Lugano.

Participants documented thermal conditions encountered along predefined walking routes while exploring the relationship between urban form, environmental exposure, and public space.

## Folder Contents

### `Thermal-access_camera`

Folder containing all thermally processed images generated by the Thermal-access tool during the data collection walks.

The images combine photographic information with thermal sensor readings, producing visual heat maps that represent surface temperature variations across the observed urban environment.

### `Thermal-access_metadata.json`

Metadata file containing structured information associated with each thermal observation.  [oai_citation:0‡Thermal-access_metadata.json](sediment://file_0000000075d071f4a59609d47444e3ae)

The file includes:

- Image filename
- Timestamp
- Minimum recorded temperature (°C)
- Maximum recorded temperature (°C)
- Average recorded temperature (°C)
- Thermal sensor readings associated with each captured image

Example fields:

- `image_file`
- `timestamp`
- `sensor_readings.thermal.min_temp`
- `sensor_readings.thermal.max_temp`
- `sensor_readings.thermal.avg_temp`

The recorded observations contain thermal measurements ranging from approximately **13°C to 66°C**, depending on surface conditions, solar exposure, and time of collection.  [oai_citation:1‡Thermal-access_metadata.json](sediment://file_0000000075d071f4a59609d47444e3ae)
